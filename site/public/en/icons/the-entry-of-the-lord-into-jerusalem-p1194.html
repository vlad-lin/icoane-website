The workshop's description

The Entry into Jerusalem – icon painted on glass
An authentic, framed, traditional Romanian icon, made in the style of the icons painted by the Transylvanian icon painters of the 18th–19th centuries, respecting the techniques of this art form. Model: Arpașu de Sus, Țara Oltului, 1872 (Savu Moga)
Painted on glass in several layers, using tempera and gold leaf, over a pen-and-ink drawing made after the original model.
The ink drawing is fixed and protected with varnish, and the finished painting has a protective layer of colour with linseed oil.
The stained fir frame is also handmade.
Icon size: 30x43 cm (without frame); 37x50 cm (with frame)

The theme of this icon is "The Entry of the Lord into Jerusalem", also known as "Palm Sunday".

The Entry of the Lord into Jerusalem, also known as Palm Sunday, is one of the Great Feasts of the Orthodox Church, celebrated on the Sunday before Easter.

A few days before His crucifixion, the Lord Jesus Christ was welcomed, as the Messiah and King of Israel, by the crowds who glorified and honoured Him as He entered Jerusalem riding on the foal of a donkey. In their joy, the crowds threw palm or fig leaves before Him, the symbol of victory, and even the children cried out words of praise to Him.

Traditions
Orthodox Christians celebrate this day as a joyful one, but with the understanding that the days that follow are days of sorrow.

Orthodox Christians in countries where palms and figs do not grow, such as the Romanians or the Russians, use willow branches instead for this feast. The faithful bring to church these branches of willow, fig or palm (also called, by an older name, “stâlpări”, hence the Romanian name of the feast, “Duminica Stâlpărilor”), recalling those with which the Saviour was welcomed as He entered Jerusalem. At the end of the Holy Liturgy, the priest says a prayer of blessing over them, sprinkles them with holy water to sanctify them, and then gives them to the faithful, who adorn their icons with them and keep them with care. In some Churches it is the custom, after the Liturgy, to give out small crosses made of palm leaves, which are then placed by the icons.

On the eve of the feast, in some places a pilgrimage or procession is held, which always starts from one church and ends at (another) church. In this way the people's going out to meet the Lord on His way to Jerusalem is remembered, and the pilgrimage itself also serves to bless the place.
