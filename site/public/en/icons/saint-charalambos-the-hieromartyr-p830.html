The workshop's description · 9 February 2026

⊹10 February: «Saint Charalambos enthroned, binding the plague»

• Model: Transylvanian workshop, Laz, first half of the 19th century

• The saint is shown seated on a bishop's throne, symbolising his spiritual power and his victory over death. Under the saint's feet or beside the throne there is always a demonic figure, often winged, carrying a scythe.

• This scene reflects the popular devotion to the saint who delivered whole communities from devastating epidemics, such as the famous «Caragea's plague» in Bucharest.

• These icons were not only objects of worship; they also had a strong protective (apotropaic) role, guarding the home and the family against contagious diseases.

• Icons showing Saint Charalambos in this form are considered collector's pieces.

Other presentations of this icon

9 February 2025

• Saint Charalambos the Hieromartyr (10 February)

• On 10 February, the Romanian Orthodox Church celebrates Saint Charalambos, protector of the poor and the sick and deliverer from plague. A saint to whom the Romanian peasant (especially in 18th–19th century Transylvania) once prayed for protection from plague, cholera, typhoid fever and the other epidemics that used to ravage people. The same Romanian peasant “painted” Saint Charalambos in countless icons on glass. One of them (painted in a Transylvanian workshop in the early 19th century) is the model of the icon I am showing you today, an icon painted in our workshop.

• The icon shows Saint Charalambos binding the plague, and so saving the city. Charalambos is shown in the usual way, dressed in robe, mantle and omophorion, with the holy book and halo, giving a blessing; he tramples the plague, which is bound by a chain. The plague, carrying a scythe, has the face of a horned man.

19 August 2024

«Saint Charalambos enthroned, binding the plague» – a study after an icon made in a Transylvanian workshop in the early 19th century.

The Akathist to the Saint says: "Rejoice, deliverer from plague and famine!"

It happened in Wallachia. In 1813, more than 300 people died in Bucharest every day, when "Caragea's plague" swept away more than 90,000 people.

The Romanians' only salvation was Saint Charalambos, known in the tradition of the Romanian people as the deliverer from the plague epidemic, to whom prayers for salvation were raised day and night.

The central scene of the icon recreates the moment when Saint Charalambos binds the neck of the figure of the plague with a heavy iron chain, so that it can no longer harm people.
