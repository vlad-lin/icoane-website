The workshop's description

The Baptism of the Lord Jesus Christ – icon painted on glass

The theme of the Baptism of the Lord, one of the great events of Christendom, also called Theophany, Epiphany or the Divine Manifestation, holds an important place in the peasant icons on glass.
The icon (painted after a model from Șcheii Brașovului, 19th century) shows Jesus Christ in a humble attitude, with His hands crossed on His chest, at the moment He is baptised by His Forerunner, the great prophet John. Also in a humble attitude is an angel of the Lord, holding a towel in both hands as the Lord comes out of the waters of the Jordan.)

The icon is painted on glass in several layers, using tempera and gold leaf, over a pen-and-ink drawing made after the original model.
The ink drawing is fixed and protected with varnish, and the finished painting has a protective layer of colour with linseed oil.
The stained fir frame is also handmade.
Icon size: 21x29 cm (without frame); 27.55x35.5 cm (with frame)

Epiphany, or Theophany (from the Greek theophania, meaning “the appearance / manifestation of God”), is one of the Great Feasts of the Orthodox Church, celebrated on 6 January.
On this day we celebrate the baptism of the Lord Jesus Christ by Saint John the Baptist in the river Jordan and the beginning of Christ's early preaching. The feast of Epiphany marks the end of the Christmas celebrations, which begin on 25 December and end on 6 January. During the service of this feast, the Great Blessing of the Waters takes place; the holy water is used by priests to bless the homes of the faithful.

The feast is called Theophany because at the baptism of Christ the Holy Trinity appeared to the world for the first time: the voice of the Father is heard from heaven, the Son is incarnate and stands in the river Jordan, and the Holy Spirit descends upon Him in the form of a dove.
