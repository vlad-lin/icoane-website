The workshop's description · 9 February 2024

⚜️10 February: Saint Charalambos the Hieromartyr

“I feel comforted and strengthened knowing that I will spend the rest of my days watched over and protected with boundless tenderness by Saint Charalambos, from the window of his icon, which comes from the peasant world so dear to me. A Friend of Christ and, I hope, a friend of mine, in a constant and beneficent work…” (Costion Nicolescu)

Today we welcome the great feast with Costion Nicolescu's wonderful words about Saint Charalambos and his icon on glass – and, of course, with an icon from the peasant world made in our workshop (model: Șcheii Brașovului, 19th century).

About the icon
The icon shows Saint Charalambos binding the plague, and so saving the city.
Charalambos is shown in the usual way (standing, dressed in robe, mantle and omophorion, with the holy book and halo, giving a blessing), trampling the plague, which is bound by a chain fastened to his belt.
The plague, carrying a scythe, has the face of a horned man. On either side of the saint, in the background, the image is enriched by a cityscape with palaces and fortified walls.
The ground is rendered by bands of colour bearing hatching, crosses and flowers. From high in the sky the scene is watched over by Jesus, who blesses him, and by an angel holding out a wreath of flowers to him. They are seated on clouds. On the light-blue background are large unoutlined flowers and yellow, white and black stars (source of the description: the National Museum of Art of Romania)

⚜️Saint Charalambos the Hieromartyr (10 February)⚜️
• On 10 February the Romanian Orthodox Church celebrates Saint Charalambos, protector of the poor and the sick and deliverer from plague, a saint to whom the Romanian peasant (especially in 18th–19th century Transylvania) once prayed for protection from plague, cholera, typhoid fever and the other epidemics that used to ravage people. The same Romanian peasant “painted” Saint Charalambos in countless icons on glass. One of them (painted in Șcheii Brașovului in the 19th century) is the model of the icon I am showing you today, an icon painted in our workshop (in the traditional technique) three years ago
