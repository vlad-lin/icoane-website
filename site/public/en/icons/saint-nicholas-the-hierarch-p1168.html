Saint Nicholas – icon painted on glass

Most of the icons painted by the unique and wonderful Transylvanian icon painters of the 18th–19th centuries are made like fantastic painted fairy tales, in which the Saints "meet" harmoniously with earthly people caught in their daily activities. This icon too (model: Cârțișoara, second half of the 18th century) is like a gentle story told "by the fireside": Saint Nicholas is shown as a high prelate, holding the Scripture in his right hand and blessing with his left. Beside him are the three girls (and their father) who, according to the legend, were brought onto the right path through the help the Saint gave them. In the background (on clouds) are Jesus and the Mother of God.

Saint Nicholas, or “the Wonderworker”, celebrated by Christians on 6 December, lived in the late 3rd and early 4th centuries. He was born into a wealthy family in Patara, in the province of Lycia, in the Asian part of today's Turkey. From childhood he showed exceptional faith, and in his youth he stood out for his fine conduct, fasting, discernment and intense spiritual life. He studied at the best schools, where he stood out for his intelligence, and in the churches he entered he amazed everyone with his gentleness and nobility of soul.
Since his uncle was bishop of Patara, Nicholas spent some time at the monastery there, where he became a priest.
Saint Nicholas worked miracles even during his lifetime. On a journey to the Holy Land, a violent storm frightened those on the ship, but Nicholas urged everyone to pray to God, and the storm stopped.
At the same time, Saint Nicholas raised a young sailor who had fallen from the mast and drowned. In Alexandria, too, Saint Nicholas healed many who were sick or possessed.
He saved the city of Myra (also in Lycia) from famine by appearing in a dream to an Italian merchant and urging him to come and sell his wheat there.
Because of the miracles he worked, Saint Nicholas is considered the protector of children, especially poor children, and also of sailors, bakers, unmarried girls and the unjustly accused.

Saint Nicholas is celebrated on 6 December by both the Orthodox and the Roman Catholic Church.

The relics of Saint Nicholas are kept in the Basilica of Bari, in Italy, in a crypt from which a colourless liquid flows, called “Manna” or “Holy Manna”. According to tradition, the bishop of this diocese mixes this liquid with water, blesses it and gives it to the faithful.
Part of the relics of Saint Nicholas are at the church of Saint George the New in Bucharest, at kilometre zero. Many places of worship also bear the name of Saint Nicholas.
