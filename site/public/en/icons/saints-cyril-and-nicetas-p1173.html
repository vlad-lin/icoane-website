~ Icon painted on glass (Saint Cyril and Saint Nicetas) ~
> Orthodox calendar, 18 March 2022. It is a feast day on which Saint Cyril is remembered and his prayer to the Holy Trinity for the enlightenment of the mind and the healing of the body is said!
(I painted the two saints together in an icon ordered for two little brothers.)
> An article by Fr. Ștefan Sfarghie – 18 March 2022:
Saint Cyril (†386) came from the region of Jerusalem and was born in the time of Emperor Constantine the Great (306–337) to Christian parents. He was 12 years old at the time of the First Ecumenical Council of Nicaea, in 325. In those days, the place of Golgotha and the wood of the Holy Cross were being discovered in Jerusalem, and by order of the emperor the Church of the Holy Sepulchre and the Church of the Resurrection were being built. Saint Cyril served in the Church of the Resurrection in his youth, and it was in this church that he delivered his famous Catecheses, or Lectures, preparing for baptism those who wished to become Christians. Around the year 350, when his bishop Maximus died, Saint Cyril was chosen Bishop of the Holy City. The following year the sign of the Holy Cross appeared in the sky over Jerusalem, and the saint informed Emperor Constantius about it in detail. Then hard days began for the Church of Christ, and the Arian emperors Constantius and Valens, together with the Arian bishops, managed twice to remove Saint Cyril, the defender of the apostolic dogmas, from his see. After more than 16 years of exile, towards the end of his life, Saint Cyril returned to his diocese. Shepherding his rational flock with love and leaving the Church the treasure of his 24 Catecheses and the example of his life, he fell asleep in the Lord after taking part in the Second Ecumenical Council of Constantinople in 381.

18 January 2022

Two Saints are depicted in this icon painted on glass: Saint Cyril and Saint Nicetas. Why together? For the simple reason that icons painted on glass are original and special gifts, and this icon was (and is) a special gift for two little brothers.
Happy name day to all who bear the name of Saint Cyril (celebrated today)!

Cyril of Alexandria was Patriarch of Alexandria, a prolific writer and the main protagonist of the Christological controversies of the 4th and 5th centuries. He was the central figure of the Council of Ephesus in 431, which ended with the removal of Nestorius from the see of Archbishop of Constantinople. Saint Cyril is one of the Fathers of the Church, and his fame in the Orthodox world earned him the name “Seal of all the Fathers”.
He is celebrated both on 9 June and on 18 January, together with Saint Athanasius of Alexandria.

Saint Nicetas the Confessor was born in Caesarea of Bithynia, in Asia Minor. As an adult he entered the Monastery of Saint Sergius of Medikion, on Mount Olympus. He was ordained a priest, and in the year 800, after the death of Abbot Nikephoros, Saint Nicetas was made abbot. Under the iconoclast emperor Leo the Armenian (813–820), because of the steadfastness with which he defended the holy icons, Saint Nicetas was imprisoned, subjected to terrible torture and finally confined on the island of Saint Glyceria, in the Sea of Marmara, where he was tormented for six years. After the death of Emperor Leo the Armenian, the persecution of the Christians who venerated the holy icons ceased and the prisoners were freed. Saint Nicetas did not return to his monastery, but joined the community of a hermitage near Constantinople, where he departed to eternal life on 3 April 824. He is celebrated on 3 April, together with Saint Illyricus.
